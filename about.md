<div class="about-page">
  <h1>About this site</h1>
  <p class="about-meta">A personal project, last updated October 2026.</p>

  <div class="about-section">
    <h2>Who runs it</h2>
    <p>This site is maintained by an overseas Chinese family in the US. We have spent several years figuring out how to watch Chinese-language TV (CCTV, Phoenix, TVB, regional channels) reliably from North America — both for ourselves and for our parents who refuse to learn yet another app.</p>
    <p>Every guide on this site comes from real, paid, hands-on testing. We pay for every service we review with our own wallet. We do not accept sponsorship, paid placement, or free trial extensions in exchange for positive coverage.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="about-section">
    <h2>What this site is not</h2>
    <p>This site does not host, stream, or rebroadcast any TV channel. It does not sell M3U subscriptions directly. It is a setup guide, not a content provider.</p>
    <p>Every article explicitly states the factual limits of each option (which channels are included, which are not, what the actual monthly cost is). We do not evaluate the copyright status of any specific service's content source — that is the responsibility of each service and the user who subscribes to it.</p>
    <p>When you use our remote help, we operate your device and nothing else. We do not touch your accounts, we do not keep your credentials, and we do not store anything on your device beyond the setup you asked for.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="about-section">
    <h2>How we test</h2>
    <p>When we test a service, we subscribe with a real credit card. We run the subscription through a real Android TV box (Fire TV Stick 4K Max) and a real iPhone. We record actual channel lineups, actual loading times, and actual buffering frequency during peak hours (weekend 8-10 PM US Pacific).</p>
    <p>Each guide lists the test date in the meta line. Prices and channel lineups change often — always confirm on the service's official page before subscribing.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="about-section">
    <h2>The commercial mention</h2>
    <p>If you scroll to the bottom of any article, you will see a single link to a paid service. That service is operated by the same author. The link is short, factual, and labeled. We are transparent about it because:</p>
    <ul>
      <li>You came here to solve a real problem, not to read an ad.</li>
      <li>Some readers would rather pay for the service to skip the 30-minute setup than DIY it. That is a valid choice.</li>
      <li>Maintaining this site takes a few hours per article (subscribe, test, screenshot, write, edit). The referral link covers the hosting and testing costs.</li>
    </ul>
    <p>If you would rather configure the setup yourself, every guide gives you the complete steps to do so. The DIY path is the primary content of this site.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="about-section">
    <h2>Editorial process</h2>
    <p>Every article follows the same structure: problem framing (what does the user actually need), options compared (real prices, real hardware requirements), one recommended technical path, FAQ, conclusion. The 5-section structure is deliberate — it lets you skip to the section you care about without reading the whole article.</p>
    <p>Setup guides follow a longer structure: the devices we support, what you need ready before you start, the steps themselves (one screenshot and at most three lines per step), a troubleshooting list of the eight failures we have actually hit, and a link to the main site for the service part — this site itself only carries the tutorial.</p>
    <p>Articles are rewritten (not just updated) when the underlying technology changes materially. The most recent rewrite was October 2026, after we noticed Fire TV Stick 4K Max dropped to $89.48 and several M3U players added new features.</p>
  </div>
